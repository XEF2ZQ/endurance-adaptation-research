# Audit of the starting analysis

This table summarizes and corrects claims; it does not reproduce the originating conversation. Reference numbers refer to the article. The exposure scenarios are hypothetical and are not attributed to an identifiable individual's health.

| Starting claim | Assessment | Replacement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2,000-3,000 ng/dL necessarily produces a faster hybrid athlete than 600-700 | Unsupported certainty | Effects on muscle and blood do not determine the net event outcome. [1,2,4,5] |
| Increasing from 2,000 to 3,000 scales the aerobic advantage | Unidentified dose response | No matching trial supplies this concentration-to-adaptation function. |
| Serum testosterone uniquely determines training exposure | Incomplete | Sampling time, exposure duration, binding proteins and the rest of the physiological state matter. |
| 2-3 g/dL describes the intended testosterone concentrations | Unit error | The intended units are ng/dL; g/dL differs by a factor of one billion. |
| Estradiol of 30 pg/mL is deficient relative to high testosterone | Unsupported classification | No validated endurance-specific ratio establishes deficiency; that value overlaps baseline means in healthy study participants. [3] |
| A particular testosterone-to-estradiol ratio is optimal | Unsupported | Ratio depends on units; neither mass nor molar ratio supplies an endurance target. |
| Estradiol was artificially clamped | Not established | A hypothetical stable concentration or low aromatization does not establish an intervention. |
| Estradiol of 30 causes impaired GLUT4 and calf fuel shortage | Unsupported individual mechanism | Exercise-associated GLUT4 translocation is established; the proposed serum-to-flux deficit is not. [6] |
| Calf fatigue directly diagnoses endocrine fuel mismatch | Invalid inference | Local load, mechanics and systemic demand need independent assessment. |
| High testosterone ensures high hematocrit | Overstatement | Erythropoiesis is supported; the individual's hematological response must be measured. [1,2] |
| More hemoglobin must improve oxygen delivery and endurance | Incomplete | Cardiac output, extraction, body mass and economy also enter the performance problem. |
| High testosterone and estradiol of 30 imply vasoconstricted calves | Unsupported | Microvascular behavior was not measured. |
| Hormonal state makes clearance linear rather than exponential | Unsupported and internally inconsistent | No clearance measurements were supplied; the original code itself uses an exponential. |
| Lactate or structural waste needs flushing | Incorrect mechanism | Lactate is an exchanged metabolic substrate; fatigue cannot be reduced to waste clearance. [16] |
| Working below threshold is entirely aerobic | Incorrect dichotomy | Oxidative and glycolytic pathways coexist; threshold concerns response behavior. [12,16] |
| CP can be sustained indefinitely without fatigue | Incorrect interpretation | CP describes an intensity boundary and model asymptote, not unlimited exercise duration. [12] |
| Cycling sweet-spot percentages map directly to rope cadence | Invalid transfer | Modality and threshold method require separate calibration. [9,10] |
| CNS drive or aggression establishes greater CP | Unsupported endpoint substitution | CP requires appropriate exercise measurement; arousal is not CP. |
| Muscle protein synthesis operates at maximum at these concentrations | Unsupported saturation claim | No relevant maximum or adaptation function was measured. [1] |
| Microdamage repairs almost instantly, eliminating deload needs | Unsupported | Lean-mass changes do not establish complete muscle or connective-tissue recovery. |
| High testosterone guarantees a massive glycogen tank | Unquantified extrapolation | Measure or appropriately estimate substrate status; do not assign stores from serum testosterone. |
| Two or three minutes restores glycogen sufficiently by hormonal design | Incorrect timescale inference | Recovery duration and nutrition matter; brief recovery is not full glycogen restoration. [7,18] |
| Estradiol of 30 prevents edema or implies dry joints | Unsupported | No such guarantee follows from one serum concentration. |
| Seven weekly hours are automatically absorbed at high testosterone | Unsupported | Load tolerance requires observed recovery and local tolerance. |
| Mitochondrial adaptation can be ranked from the hormone pair | Unidentified | No longitudinal mitochondrial or aerobic data were supplied. |
| 85-88% of threshold maximizes stroke volume and capillary adaptation | Unsupported optimum and ambiguous units | Specify the threshold and modality, then measure outcomes. |
| Six to eight minutes is the exact best interval length | Unsupported | A candidate duration is not an identified optimum. |
| Fixed three-minute rests are uniquely required | Contradicted by the supplied score | Its grid maximum uses four minutes; there is no fitted recovery constant. |
| Forty minutes is a universal depletion and adaptation boundary | Unsupported discontinuity | No matching data establish a sharp cutoff. |
| Double sessions necessarily outperform one longer session | Unsupported generalization | Compare matched weekly work and account for recovery, logistics and adaptation. |
| The score predicts adaptation per unit time | Mathematical labeling error | It scores one bout; it never divides by duration or models the weekly program. |
| The score optimizes intensity | False | Intensity is fixed at 0.88. |
| The score is specific to testosterone and estradiol | False | Hormones occur in comments, not in the input-dependent equation. |
| The score identifies a physiological optimum | False | Coefficients have no calibration, outcome units, uncertainty estimate or validation set. |

The replacement model intentionally stops at exact scheduling and explicitly hypothetical acute response calculations. It returns no predicted change in VO2peak, CP, race time or injury probability.
